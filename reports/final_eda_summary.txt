
HR ANALYTICS PROJECT
Final Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) Summary

1. PROJECT OVERVIEW
This project analyzes employee attrition using the IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition & Performance dataset.

The objective is to identify employee attrition patterns, understand factors associated with employees leaving the organization, and provide data-driven recommendations to improve employee retention.

Tools Used:
- PostgreSQL
- SQL
- Python
- Pandas
- Matplotlib
- Seaborn
- VS Code

2. DATASET OVERVIEW
- Total Employees: 1,470
- Employees Who Stayed: 1,233
- Employees Who Left: 237
- Total Columns: 32
- Overall Attrition Rate: 16.12%

3. KEY FINDINGS

A. Department-wise Attrition
- Sales: 20.63%
- Human Resources: 19.05%
- Research & Development: 13.84%

Sales has the highest attrition rate among the three departments.

D. Overtime Analysis

4. IMPORTANT BUSINESS INSIGHTS

1. Sales Representatives are a high-priority group for employee retention.
2. Younger employees, particularly those under 25, show higher attrition.
3. Employees working overtime have a considerably higher attrition rate.
4. Lower job satisfaction and poor work-life balance are associated with higher attrition.
5. Frequent business travel and longer commuting distances are associated with higher attrition.
6. Employees who left had lower average monthly income than employees who stayed.

5. BUSINESS RECOMMENDATIONS

1. Review workload distribution and overtime policies.
2. Introduce employee engagement and retention programs for younger employees.
3. Review compensation and career growth opportunities.
4. Improve work-life balance through flexible working arrangements where possible.
5. Evaluate travel requirements and employee commuting challenges.
6. Conduct regular employee satisfaction surveys.
7. Develop targeted retention strategies for high-attrition job roles.

7. FINAL CONCLUSION

The analysis identified several employee groups with comparatively higher attrition rates, particularly Sales Representatives, employees under 25, and employees working overtime.

Job satisfaction, work-life balance, income, business travel, and commuting distance also showed associations with employee attrition.

These insights can help HR teams identify retention priorities and develop data-driven employee engagement strategies.

Note: The analysis identifies associations and patterns in the dataset. It does not establish that any individual factor directly causes employee attrition.
